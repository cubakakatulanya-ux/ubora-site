Ubora VSLA

Strong savings groups, and real access to credit.

For many Congolese families, the VSLA is their first financial service. We support the group from its creation to its first loan.

The context in the DRC

What we see in the field

The VSLA fills the gap left by the absence of banks, with very simple means. But most groups remain fragile.

What is a VSLA?

Fifteen to thirty people who save every week and grant each other small loans. At the end of the cycle, often after nine to twelve months, the fund is shared out. A social fund helps in hard times.

  • The record book gets lost or damagedOne copying error, and trust erodes when the fund is shared out.
  • Years of savings with no recordA reliable group often has nothing to show a bank.
  • Support ends with the projectWithout supervision, many groups break up.
Our method

Step by step

  1. 01

    Assess

    Baseline study and mapping of existing groups.

  2. 02

    Set up

    Forming the group, statutes, rules, electing the committee.

  3. 03

    Train

    Members, group facilitators and local community facilitators, financial education included.

  4. 04

    Digitise

    From record book to AKIBA: guided meetings, sealed register, calculated share-out.

  5. 05

    Link to finance

    The track record presented to MFIs, COOPECs and banks; capital mobilised by Ubora Fin.

  6. 06

    Monitor and grow

    Supervision, graduation, income-generating activities, federation.
